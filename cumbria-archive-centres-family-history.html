Archive research guide

Cumbria Archive Centres
for Family Historians

Cumbria Archives has four archive centres. Choosing the right centre starts with the historic location and the type of record you need.

☙
Home/Guides/Cumbria Archive Centres for Family History Research

By Brittany Warren · Updated 24 July 2026

Cumbria Archives cares for records from the Middle Ages to the present day through centres at Carlisle, Whitehaven, Kendal and Barrow. Their holdings reflect historic counties and local collecting areas, so the nearest modern town is not always the only relevant place to search.

Carlisle Archive Centre

Carlisle is a key starting point for northern and eastern Cumberland, including Carlisle and many surrounding communities. Collections may include parish, local government, court, estate, business and personal records. Original documents generally require advance planning and ordering.

Whitehaven Archive Centre

Whitehaven is particularly important for west Cumbria, including records connected with Whitehaven, Workington, Cockermouth and surrounding areas. Mining, shipping, industry, estates and coastal communities can create valuable local sources beyond parish and census records.

Kendal Archive Centre

Kendal holds records relating to the historic county of Westmorland and also material for the Sedbergh-Dent area and Cartmel district. These changing jurisdictions are especially important when a birthplace or parish seems to move between counties.

Barrow Archive Centre

Barrow is relevant to south Cumbria and the Furness area, including places historically described as Lancashire North of the Sands. Industrial, shipping, local-government and community collections can be particularly valuable.

Before an archive visit

  • Define the research question and known date range
  • Search the online catalogue and note exact references
  • Check current opening and booking arrangements
  • Order documents in advance where required
  • Bring identification and follow handling rules
  • Plan alternative records in case the first source is inconclusive

When professional archive research helps

Archive catalogues often describe collections rather than naming every person within them. I can identify likely record series, interpret older handwriting, correlate findings with online evidence and document the result clearly.

My experience: I have spent substantial time working with Cumbria Archives and researching the local record systems of Cumberland, Westmorland and modern Cumbria.

Always check the official Cumbria Archives visitor information before travelling, because access arrangements can change.
