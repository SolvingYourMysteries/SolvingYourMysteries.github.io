Cumbria Archive Centres
for Family Historians

Cumbria Archives has four archive centres. Choosing the right centre starts with the historic location and the type of record you need.

Home/Guides/Cumbria Archive Centres for Family History Research

By Brittany Warren · Updated 24 July 2026

Cumbria Archives has centres at Carlisle, Whitehaven, Kendal and Barrow, with collections ranging from medieval material to modern local records. Each centre has its own collecting area, shaped partly by historic county boundaries, so it is worth checking more than the nearest present-day town.

Carlisle Archive Centre

Carlisle is often the first centre to check for northern and eastern Cumberland. Its collections include material created by parishes, local authorities, courts, estates, businesses and private individuals. Many original documents need to be ordered in advance.

Whitehaven Archive Centre

Whitehaven serves much of west Cumbria, including Whitehaven, Workington, Cockermouth and surrounding communities. For families connected with mining, shipping, industry or local estates, the archive can hold records that never appear in a census or parish database.

Kendal Archive Centre

Kendal is central to research in historic Westmorland and also holds material for the Sedbergh-Dent area and the Cartmel district. Those collections are especially useful when a place appears under different counties at different dates.

Barrow Archive Centre

Barrow is relevant to south Cumbria and the Furness area, including places historically described as Lancashire North of the Sands. Industrial, shipping, local-government and community collections can be particularly valuable.

Before an archive visit

  • Define the research question and known date range
  • Search the online catalogue and note exact references
  • Check current opening and booking arrangements
  • Order documents in advance where required
  • Bring identification and follow handling rules
  • Plan alternative records in case the first source is inconclusive

When professional archive research helps

Many archive catalogues describe a box, volume or estate collection without listing every person named inside it. I can help identify likely series, read older handwriting and connect what is found in the archive with the family evidence already available online or at home.

My experience: I have spent substantial time working with Cumbria Archives and researching the local record systems of Cumberland, Westmorland and modern Cumbria.

Always check the official Cumbria Archives visitor information before travelling, because access arrangements can change.
