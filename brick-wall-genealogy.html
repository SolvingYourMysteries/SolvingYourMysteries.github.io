Complex case service

Genealogy Brick Wall
Research

A fresh, systematic investigation for ancestors who disappear, change identity or resist the usual searches.

Home/Services/Genealogy Brick Wall Research

When the usual searches have stopped producing anything useful, I go back to the point where the evidence is still secure. From there I rebuild the timeline, check earlier assumptions and look at the relatives, places and record sets that may have been overlooked.

Problems that often need a fresh review

  • An ancestor disappears between censuses
  • A birthplace, age or surname changes repeatedly
  • No convincing birth or marriage record can be found
  • Several people share the same name and occupation
  • Online trees repeat a connection without evidence
  • A family crossed county or national borders

Bring the earlier research with you

Please send the records behind the current conclusion: certificates, images, citations, tree links, previous reports and any notes on searches already tried. Knowing where other researchers have looked is often as useful as knowing what they found. I will also ask you to choose the main question for the first stage.

How I reopen a difficult case

I build a chronology first and mark the places where the identity, relationship or date becomes uncertain. The next search may follow a sibling, witness, neighbour, employer or address; in other cases a migration route or DNA match provides the better lead. The method depends on where the original chain of evidence breaks.

Records and platforms

The sources depend on the problem and may include civil and parish records, census returns, probate, deeds, newspapers, military files, immigration records, court material, archive catalogues, Ancestry, FamilySearch, GEDmatch and specialist regional collections. A source is useful only when its coverage and limitations fit the question.

Scope, timing and budget

A narrow record check or review of one contradiction may fit the Quick Search. A longstanding identity, migration or parentage problem is charged at £35 per hour and will normally begin with a five-hour stage because several hypotheses and related people may need to be investigated.

The timetable depends on the number of places involved, archive access, record survival and the volume of earlier research that must be checked. I estimate the first stage after reviewing the material you already have.

The case file you receive

The agreed output may include a written analysis of the existing evidence, a chronology, research log, source references, newly found records, the hypotheses tested, a conclusion proportionate to the evidence and recommendations for the next stage.

When a search result is 'nothing found'

A well-defined unsuccessful search can be useful. If a complete register covers the right place and years and the expected entry is absent, that may change the theory of the case. I record those searches so the same ground does not have to be covered again.

Where a brick-wall project may end

A project may solve the identity, leave one explanation clearly stronger than the others, or show that a crucial record has not survived. The report states where the evidence has reached and which remaining search is most likely to be worthwhile.

Three very different brick walls

My James Forster of Lanercost case study shows how a missing baptism, exact date and surname change produced a strong new theory through archive research. The Louisa Gierschick case study demonstrates why a record not found online may still survive with a local custodian. The Henry Logan case study combines migration, same-name problems and DNA across the United States and Canada.
