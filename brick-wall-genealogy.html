Home/Services/Genealogy Brick Wall Research

A brick wall is rarely solved by repeating the same name search. Difficult cases require a clear research question, a complete review of existing evidence and a plan that tests several possible explanations.

Typical brick walls

  • An ancestor disappears between censuses
  • A birthplace, age or surname changes repeatedly
  • No convincing birth or marriage record can be found
  • Several people share the same name and occupation
  • Online trees repeat a connection without evidence
  • A family crossed county or national borders

A systematic method

I start by auditing what is already known, identifying assumptions and constructing timelines. I may research siblings, neighbours, witnesses, employers, addresses and wider family networks; compare naming patterns; examine migration routes; or use DNA matches to test documentary hypotheses.

Negative evidence matters

Research is not only a list of records found. Searches that produce no result can narrow the possibilities when the collection, date range and expected coverage are properly understood. Reports document both positive and relevant negative findings.

A real Cumberland brick wall

My James Forster of Lanercost case study shows this process in practice: an ancestor had a clear adult paper trail but no matching baptism under the surname Forster. An archive visit, an exact date and the repeated use of the name Nelson created a strong alternative identity without overstating what the evidence could prove.

Realistic outcomes

Some cases produce a strong answer, others identify the most likely explanation, and some remain unresolved because records did not survive. You receive an honest assessment and clear next steps rather than a forced conclusion.
