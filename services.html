Adoption & Unknown Parentage

These cases require discretion and methodical evidence-building — whether you're looking for a biological parent or want to understand an adoption from a hundred years ago.

I use all the resources at my disposal to piece together the strongest possible case supported by verifiable records. Each situation is unique, and I approach every case with sensitivity and a commitment to following the evidence wherever it leads.

I build carefully structured cases using:

  • DNA clustering techniques
  • Family tree reconstruction of matches
  • Timeline and geographic comparison
  • Civil registration and corroborating documentation
  • Indirect evidence and elimination methodology

02

Genetic Genealogy

DNA is a powerful research tool — not only for adoption cases, but for solving complex family questions that span generations.

I analyse DNA matches to uncover patterns that traditional records cannot reveal. Genetic genealogy is most effective when combined with careful documentary research, and I always use both in tandem to reach the strongest possible conclusions.

DNA analysis can help to:

  • Break through brick walls
  • Confirm or challenge existing research
  • Identify shared ancestors
  • Reconstruct extended family networks
  • Support documentary findings with genetic evidence

03

Document-Based Research

Traditional archival research forms the foundation of my work. I locate and evaluate original records to build conclusions that can be trusted.

I conduct a thorough investigation of primary sources across a wide range of archive types and collections, both national and local. Every record I cite is properly sourced so you always know the basis for each finding.

Records I work with include:

  • Civil registrations (birth, marriage, death)
  • Parish and church records
  • Probate records and wills
  • Census and electoral rolls
  • Military, land, and court records
  • Newspaper archives
  • Immigration and passenger lists
  • Local archival collections

04

Brick Wall & Complex Cases

I re-evaluate evidence, reconstruct family networks, and test multiple hypotheses to break through difficult research problems.

Brick wall cases demand a different kind of thinking: lateral, systematic, and patient. Rather than repeating the same searches, I step back, map the problem carefully, and work through the most promising alternative avenues to find a way forward.

My approach includes:

  • Reconstructing entire family networks to identify one individual
  • Analysing migration patterns
  • Identifying naming traditions and kinship structures
  • Evaluating conflicting or incomplete records
  • Developing and testing multiple research hypotheses

05

International & Multi-Regional Research

I have conducted genealogical research across multiple regions, each with its own archival systems, languages, and historical complexities.

International research requires an understanding of how different record-keeping systems developed, what survives, and where it is held. My experience spans several continents, and I am comfortable navigating the particular challenges each region presents.

Regions covered include, but are not limited to:

  • North America (including Mexico)
  • Puerto Rico and parts of the Caribbean
  • Western Europe
  • Select regions of Eastern Europe (depending on record survival)
  • Australia
  • New Zealand

Historical Context & Narrative Reporting

Genealogy is about people, not just data. My reports place individuals within their historical world so that the findings come alive.

Wherever possible, I contextualise research findings with historical background, helping you to understand not just who your ancestors were, but the world they inhabited and the circumstances that shaped their lives and decisions.

Reports may include:

  • Social and economic background
  • Occupational research
  • Geographic and community context
  • Historical events influencing family movement
  • Clearly written research summaries with full citations

07

Family Tree Building

Whether you’re starting from scratch or working with an existing tree, building an accurate family history requires more than simply collecting names.

I help bring clarity and structure to your research, whether that means building a tree from the ground up or untangling one that has become unclear or inconsistent. My focus is always on creating a family tree that is accurate, well-supported, and easy to understand.

I assist with:

  • Building family trees using reliable, verifiable records
  • Reviewing existing trees for errors, inconsistencies, or unsupported connections
  • Organising and restructuring complex or disordered research
  • Adding clear source citations and documentation
  • Identifying gaps and recommending next steps for further research
Read the Full Family Tree GuideEnquire About This Service
08

Lineage Verification

Whether you’re confirming a direct ancestral line, preparing for an application, or ensuring your existing research is accurate, I provide careful, evidence-based verification of lineage. My goal is to confirm each generational link using reliable records and clearly documented reasoning.

I work systematically to evaluate existing research, identify gaps or inconsistencies, and build a supported line of descent that can be confidently relied upon.

I verify lineage using:

  • Civil registration records and parish registers
  • Census and household records
  • Probate, land, and legal documents
  • Correlation of multiple independent sources
  • Resolution of conflicting evidence
  • Clear documentation and source citation
