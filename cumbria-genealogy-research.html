Home/Cumbria genealogy research

Researching a family from Cumbria often means looking beyond the modern county name. Depending on the place and period, records may be catalogued under historic Cumberland, Westmorland, Lancashire North of the Sands, the West Riding of Yorkshire or neighbouring Scottish and Northumbrian jurisdictions.

Experienced research in Cumbria’s archives

I have extensive hands-on experience working with Cumbria Archives and researching Cumbrian families. I am comfortable planning searches across local catalogues, identifying the most relevant record series and connecting evidence found in parish, civil, probate, estate, occupational and community records.

Cumbria Archives operates archive centres at Carlisle, Whitehaven, Kendal and Barrow. Their collections cover different parts of the county and its historic jurisdictions. Research can therefore require careful decisions about which centre, catalogue and collection is most likely to contain the evidence.

Local research areas include: Carlisle, Brampton, Penrith, Keswick, Cockermouth, Workington, Whitehaven, Maryport, Appleby-in-Westmorland, Kendal, Ambleside, Ulverston, Barrow-in-Furness and surrounding villages.

Records that may help solve your question

  • Church of England parish registers and bishops’ transcripts
  • Methodist, Quaker, Roman Catholic and other nonconformist records
  • Birth, marriage and death certificates
  • Census returns, electoral registers and local directories
  • Wills, administrations, inventories and estate papers
  • Poor-law, workhouse, settlement and removal records
  • Quarter sessions, court and coroner material where available
  • School, business, industrial, military and occupational records
  • Historic newspapers, maps, photographs and local studies collections

Cumberland, Westmorland and border families

Families did not remain neatly within modern boundaries. Movement between Cumberland and Dumfriesshire, Northumberland, County Durham, Lancashire and Westmorland was common. Agricultural work, mining, railways, shipping, military service and marriage could all move a family across a county or national border.

A local investigation therefore considers neighbouring parishes, variant place names, changing administrative boundaries, occupational communities and extended family networks rather than searching one surname in isolation.

What you receive

Research is tailored to your question. You receive a clear account of the searches undertaken, the evidence found, source references, an explanation of any uncertainty and practical recommendations for the next stage.
