Home/Cumbria genealogy research

Researching a family from Cumbria often means looking beyond the modern county name. Depending on the place and period, records may be catalogued under historic Cumberland, Westmorland, Lancashire North of the Sands, the West Riding of Yorkshire or neighbouring Scottish and Northumbrian jurisdictions.

What kind of archive work does the question need?

Archive work can be as small as checking one known document or as involved as following a family through several collections. I scope the visit around what is already known, what can be checked online first and which original records are worth ordering.

A known source

Record or catalogue-reference lookup

Best when you already know the archive centre, collection or catalogue reference and want a particular document checked. Where archive rules permit, the result can include images; otherwise I provide a transcription or summary and the full archival reference.

A defined question

Focused archive visit

Suitable for a specific question that may require several related records, such as a parish-register entry, will, settlement paper or estate record. Preparation, document ordering and the searches undertaken are recorded clearly.

A wider investigation

Full Cumbria research case

For problems that need online preparation and archive work together. This may combine parish, civil, probate, estate, occupational, newspaper or DNA evidence to test more than one explanation.

Timing and additional costs: archive work is scheduled around centre opening arrangements, advance document ordering and the availability of original material. I confirm an expected timetable in the quote. Any separate archive reproduction, certificate, licensing or travel costs that may apply are identified before work begins.

A tightly defined lookup may fit the Quick Search. Wider cases are charged at £35 per hour and will usually start with a five-hour research stage; archive visits are scoped after I have reviewed the catalogue, the records already available and the research objective.

Working with Cumbria's archive collections

I regularly research Cumbrian families through Cumbria Archives, using the catalogues to plan work before a visit and linking archive material with parish, civil, probate, estate, occupational and community records found elsewhere.

Cumbria Archives operates archive centres at Carlisle, Whitehaven, Kendal and Barrow. Their collections cover different parts of the county and its historic jurisdictions. Research can therefore require careful decisions about which centre, catalogue and collection is most likely to contain the evidence.

Local research areas include: Carlisle, Brampton, Penrith, Keswick, Cockermouth, Workington, Whitehaven, Maryport, Appleby-in-Westmorland, Kendal, Ambleside, Ulverston, Barrow-in-Furness and surrounding villages.

Records I may look for

  • Church of England parish registers and bishops’ transcripts
  • Methodist, Quaker, Roman Catholic and other nonconformist records
  • Birth, marriage and death certificates
  • Census returns, electoral registers and local directories
  • Wills, administrations, inventories and estate papers
  • Poor-law, workhouse, settlement and removal records
  • Quarter sessions, court and coroner material where available
  • School, business, industrial, military and occupational records
  • Historic newspapers, maps, photographs and local studies collections

Families rarely stayed inside one county

Families did not remain neatly within modern boundaries. Movement between Cumberland and Dumfriesshire, Northumberland, County Durham, Lancashire and Westmorland was common. Agricultural work, mining, railways, shipping, military service and marriage could all move a family across a county or national border.

That movement means a search may need to follow neighbouring parishes, old place names, changing boundaries, employers and extended family members on both sides of a county or national border.

After the archive work

I return a record of the searches made, the documents found and the archive references needed to locate them again. Where a conclusion remains uncertain, the report explains why. If an archive does not permit photography, I provide the best transcription or summary allowed under its rules.

What helps me prepare before a visit

  • The ancestor’s full name and known name variants
  • Approximate dates, places and relationships
  • Copies or links for records you already have
  • Any catalogue references or archive-centre information
  • A clear statement of the question you want the search to answer
